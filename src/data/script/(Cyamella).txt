

(CYAMELLA)


'Cyamella' designates the sesamoid bones sitting at the level of the tendons of termination of the hamstring muscles, at the back of the knee joint, near the start of the two heads of origin of the gastrocnemius muscle.

The cyamella would protect the tendons against the stresses and friction exerted during rapid flexion/extension movements (such as to escape a predator...).

It is estimated that about 10% of individuals have this vestige of evolution, especially in certain mammals, and which is not at all pathological.

In humans, these sesamoid bones would have disappeared due to the relatively slow movements of this joint.
